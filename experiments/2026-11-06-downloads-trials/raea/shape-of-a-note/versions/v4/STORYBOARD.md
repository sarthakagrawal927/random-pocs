# The shape of a note

A sixty-second audiovisual lesson: the relative strengths of harmonics change the color of a periodic tone while its fundamental frequency can stay fixed. This is an additive-synthesis demonstration, not a full explanation of instrument identity. Audience: curious adult, no musical training (assumed).

Look: warm ivory paper, deep ink, Georgia display type with Arial labels. Orange fundamental, teal third harmonic, blue fifth harmonic. Continuous waveform is the visual motif. Motion explains a cause, then holds its result. No decorative particles, neon, fake instruments, or unsupported claims.

| Time | On screen and reading order | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7 | The shape of a note. One pitch can have many colors. A single wave opens into two different profiles. | Same short phrase in a mellow and bright harmonic recipe. |
| 7–18 | 1. Start with one wave. A 220 Hz pure tone; one circle becomes a sine wave. Visual motion slowed. | Pure 220 Hz sine tone, gently gated. |
| 18–32 | 2. Add harmonics. 220, 660 and 1100 Hz enter in order. Component waves feed their sum. | Fundamental, then third, then fifth; exact frequency ratios 1:3:5. |
| 32–45 | 3. Change the recipe. The fundamental stays at 220 Hz. Relative amplitudes change; harmonics soften and brighten the tone. Small spectrum bars mirror the recipe. | Alternating soft and bright additive tones at matched RMS, with smooth crossfades. |
| 45–56 | 4. Keep the recipe. Move the notes. All partials follow the same melody; spectrum and trace move together. | A short original phrase, one voice built from the same three harmonics. |
| 56–64 | Pitch: how fast the pattern repeats. Tone color: how its harmonics are mixed. Harmonics are one part of timbre; attack and decay matter too. Ending returns to one wave. | Resolve to a soft A3, fade fully before end. |

Every scene has at least one long hold for the meaning. Readable labels stay within 120px safe margins. Curves and plots are derived from the same amplitudes and frequencies used by the sound. The animated time axis is explicitly slowed; audio is at the labeled pitch.
